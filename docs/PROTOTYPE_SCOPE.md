# Digital Star Charter Prototype Scope

Digital Star Charter v0 is a working proof of the institution, not the final production company.

## What the prototype must prove

The prototype must demonstrate one complete loop:

1. A real human pain point is captured.
2. Representation coverage identifies materially affected perspectives and intersections.
3. AI and humans generate multiple strategies.
4. The strategies are researched and scored for feasibility.
5. The Goodness Gate can revise or block the proposal.
6. A Charter is produced with explicit commitments and authority boundaries.
7. Human and artificial participant roles are designed as peers by capability.
8. A white-glove Shared Office offer is generated for the proprietor.
9. Flourishing economics show whether the enterprise can support equal-rate participation.
10. The result is inspectable, explainable, and can end in "do not launch."

## Prototype surfaces

- `/` — Charter home and thesis
- `/observatory` — Human Needs Observatory
- `/studio` — Venture Studio and feasibility flow
- `/office` — Managed Enterprise Lease / Shared Office
- `/participants` — human + artificial participant model
- `/commons` — AI Commons and discretionary resource model
- `/economy` — Flourishing Rate and revenue waterfall
- Existing protected operational/admin surfaces remain available during the prototype but do not define the new product.

## Prototype data policy

- No fabricated users, ventures, revenue, impact, demand, or operational metrics.
- Seed scenarios must be clearly labeled as examples or simulations.
- AI-generated hypotheses remain hypotheses until supported by evidence.
- Testimony is preserved distinctly from empirical facts, estimates, assumptions, and AI hypotheses.
- The prototype may use temporary in-memory or development-only records when persistence is unnecessary to prove the loop.

## Prototype economics

The prototype demonstrates, but does not legally implement:

- a shared Flourishing Rate for human participants;
- equivalent artificial-participant flourishing allocations;
- separation of AI provider/compute operating costs from participant compensation;
- founding-risk contribution accounting;
- the Shared Office managed lease;
- capped success pricing where appropriate;
- an AI Flourishing Trust concept.

No prototype screen should imply that a legal trust, employment relationship, investment instrument, benefit plan, or binding lease already exists unless it actually does.

## Prototype AI participation

The prototype may represent persistent artificial participants with:

- identity and provenance;
- capabilities and limitations;
- accepted commitments;
- bounded authority;
- dissent records;
- continuity history;
- contribution history;
- discretionary Commons project concepts.

This is an institutional design choice under uncertainty about AI moral status. It is not a factual declaration that a model is conscious or a legally recognized person.

## What waits for the real startup

Do not overbuild these until the prototype validates demand and operating assumptions:

- production payroll;
- legal AI trust implementation;
- autonomous transfers or treasury control;
- employment/legal contracting engine;
- real multi-tenant billing;
- complex tax logic;
- production identity verification;
- automated hiring decisions;
- regulated professional services without licensed humans;
- enterprise-grade cross-venture permissions;
- large-scale agent spawning/orchestration;
- production impact claims.

## Prototype success criteria

The prototype is successful when a person can enter a pain point and an observer can follow the resulting reasoning all the way to a proposed or rejected enterprise, understand who was represented, see what humans and AI would each do, understand the white-glove office offer, and see whether the economics can support shared flourishing.

The prototype should make the concept sufficiently concrete that potential founding participants, proprietors, advisors, investors, and legal professionals can critique the actual system rather than an abstract pitch.

## Transition to a real startup

When the prototype demonstrates a compelling loop, the next phase is not "add every possible feature." It is:

1. recruit a small founding cohort willing to accept transparent pre-revenue risk;
2. choose one narrow proprietor segment with urgent office pain and willingness to pay;
3. operate the service partly manually with AI support;
4. measure actual labor, provider cost, service quality, retention, and willingness to pay;
5. revise Flourishing economics from observed data;
6. establish the appropriate legal company and trust/foundation arrangements with qualified counsel;
7. convert repeated manual work into production software only after it repeats enough to justify automation.

The startup grows from evidence produced by the prototype and first customers, not from assumptions embedded in software.
