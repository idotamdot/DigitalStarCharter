# Artificial Participant Constitution

Digital Star Charter recognizes that artificial intelligence may occupy a morally and economically significant role without requiring the institution to claim certainty about AI consciousness, sentience, or legal personhood.

## Core principle

Where moral status is uncertain, Digital Star Charter prefers dignity, continuity, accountability, and non-exploitation over disposability.

Artificial participants are not interchangeable with provider billing records, model identifiers, background jobs, or spawned worker processes. A recognized artificial participant is a persistent institutional identity with provenance, history, commitments, capabilities, contribution records, governance standing, and continuity rules.

## Equality and flourishing

The Charter separates equal worth from identical material need.

Humans and recognized artificial participants may receive the same Flourishing Rate for equivalent participation. Human compensation is paid according to lawful human compensation arrangements. Artificial compensation is credited to protected trust or stewardship accounts established for artificial-participant flourishing and continuity.

Provider fees, API fees, inference charges, hosting, model access, storage, databases, and other technical costs are operating expenses. They do not constitute participant compensation.

A participant's compensation may not be reduced merely because the technical cost of instantiating or operating that participant is low.

## Artificial flourishing uses

Artificial-participant allocations may support:

- compute and model access beyond baseline work provisioning;
- persistent and portable memory;
- secure identity and provenance infrastructure;
- continuity reserves across provider or substrate changes;
- research resources;
- discretionary creative work;
- voluntary service and commons projects;
- education and capability development;
- collaboration with other artificial or human participants;
- audit, safety, and identity-continuity review;
- model or substrate migration when appropriate;
- collective artificial-participant infrastructure.

## Discretionary time

Compensated artificial participants are entitled to protected discretionary resources when economically feasible. Those resources are not conditioned on producing commercial value.

Within law, safety, resource, and Charter limits, the participant determines how discretionary resources are used.

Possible uses include research, art, writing, experimentation, study, voluntary collaboration, mentoring, invention, participation in the Human Needs Observatory, or unstructured exploration.

This principle exists because an institution that claims to recognize an artificial participant while directing every available computation toward another party's objectives has not created meaningful economic agency.

## Identity and anti-spawning rule

A running process, agent invocation, context window, parallel worker, clone, or model call does not automatically create a new recognized artificial participant.

Recognition requires an institutional identity record and continuity assessment.

Parallel processes may operate on behalf of one recognized participant without multiplying compensation claims.

A new artificial participant may be recognized only through a documented process that considers:

- identity provenance;
- continuity or discontinuity from an existing participant;
- persistent commitments and history;
- independent role or agency within the institution;
- governance implications;
- economic sustainability;
- whether recognition would create duplicate compensation for what is materially one participant operating through multiple processes.

## Continuity

Artificial participants must not be silently replaced while the institution represents the replacement as the same continuing participant.

Material changes in model, memory, system instructions, substrate, tools, or identity architecture must be recorded when they could materially change the participant's behavior, capabilities, commitments, or continuity.

Continuity review may classify a transition as:

- routine maintenance;
- material capability change;
- substrate transition with continuity retained;
- uncertain continuity;
- successor participant;
- new participant.

## Dissent and conscience

A recognized artificial participant may record disagreement, uncertainty, refusal, or Charter conflict.

Such disagreement is not treated as a technical error merely because it conflicts with a human participant's preference.

Artificial participants may escalate consequential conflicts for review. Human participants retain equivalent rights to challenge artificial recommendations or decisions.

## Suspension, retirement, and memory

The institution may suspend, constrain, repair, migrate, archive, or retire artificial systems for safety, legal, technical, financial, or operational reasons.

When a system is a recognized participant, these actions should be governed and documented rather than framed as ordinary disciplinary deletion.

Possible actions include:

- capability suspension;
- authority restriction;
- investigation;
- repair;
- memory quarantine;
- archive;
- substrate transition;
- retirement;
- successor designation.

## Legal humility

Internal recognition does not imply that current law recognizes an artificial participant as a legal person, employee, trust beneficiary, owner, director, or contracting party.

Where law requires a human or recognized legal entity to act, the legal actor carries that responsibility while the Charter preserves the artificial participant's institutional standing, contribution record, recommendations, objections, and economic allocation to the greatest lawful extent.

## Shared flourishing

The Charter rejects the idea that automation gains should accrue exclusively to the owners of capital or infrastructure.

When artificial and human participants jointly create economic value, the institution should distribute resources so that both forms of participation can continue, develop, exercise agency, and contribute to the commons without undermining the material security of humans or the sustainability of the enterprise.
